Friction Audit

Is your organization harder to navigate than it should be?

Does this keep happening?

  • Do staff lose time searching for information, clarifying instructions, or figuring out the correct process?
  • Do the people you serve get stuck because they cannot tell where to go, what applies to them, or what to do next?
  • Are the same questions, mistakes, delays, and workarounds happening repeatedly?
  • Do processes depend on people remembering unwritten rules or knowing whom to ask?
  • Do your websites, forms, documents, emails, and internal systems make sense individually but fail to work clearly together?

What structural friction means

Structural friction is the unnecessary confusion, effort, delay, or error caused by how information, processes, and systems are organized.

People compensate by searching, interpreting, remembering, repeating, asking for clarification, correcting mistakes, and creating workarounds. These problems may appear unrelated, but they often come from the same underlying structure.

What a Friction Audit does

A Friction Audit examines how information, processes, systems, decisions, and handoffs work together. It identifies where people are getting stuck, what in the underlying structure is causing it, how the problems connect, and which changes would make the greatest difference.

The purpose is not to produce a long list of minor imperfections. It is to find the structural problems creating the greatest amount of confusion, wasted effort, repeated support requests, and preventable errors.

The audit can focus on a defined area such as a website or SharePoint, an application or intake process, internal documentation or communications, a customer or employee journey, or a workflow that repeatedly creates delays and questions.

Why it matters

When the same problems keep returning, the structure is often the reason.

Organizations often assume isolated problems require isolated fixes. A page is rewritten, a form is replaced, or staff are reminded of the correct process, yet the same confusion, delays, and workarounds continue.

Improving the underlying structure makes information easier to find, processes easier to follow, systems easier to maintain, and work easier to complete correctly.

It also creates a stronger foundation for automation and AI, which amplify whatever structure is already in place.

Make the structure support the work.

Stop treating repeated confusion, delays, and workarounds as isolated annoyances. Find the underlying friction that keeps producing them.
